        Task design
        
            
        
              The Iowa Speech Sample (ISS) is a short spoken-language battery (≈10 minutes) that elicits
              semantic associations, letter-based word generation, rapid naming, and face–digit matching.
              An extended version adds sentence repetition, complex semantic prompts, timed word reading,
              and simple visual/auditory reaction-time tasks.
            
        
            
          
              
        • Semantic fluency: 12‑second prompts (e.g., street, garden) where participants say
                  as many related words as possible.
          • 
              
        • Phonemic fluency (COWAT-style): 30‑second trials generating words beginning with letters
                  A, S, C, F, and L.
          • 
              
        • Rapid Automatized Naming (RAN): reading number grids as quickly and accurately as possible
                  across short timed trials.
          • 
              
        • Face–digit matching: quickly mapping faces to digits (1–10) to probe visual–verbal mapping.
          • 
              
        • Sentence repetition (extended): repeating three sentences of increasing syntactic complexity
                  to index verbal working memory.
          • 
              
        • Complex semantic prompts, word reading, and reaction time (extended): additional prompts that
                  stress abstract semantic search, reading fluency, and simple response speed.
          • 
            
        

            
        Feature families
        
            
        
              The pipeline extracts more than 90 features per participant, grouped into several domains that
              can be viewed in the Results tab (per‑prompt, per‑task, and per‑participant files).
            
        
            
          
              
        • Productivity & lexical features: total and unique word counts, lexical diversity, age of
                  acquisition, concreteness, imageability, familiarity, and affective norms (valence, arousal).
          • 
              
        • Temporal dynamics: onset latency, inter‑word intervals, pause patterns, and response‑time
                  distribution metrics (e.g., Gini and Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistics for how evenly responses
                  are spread across the trial).
          • 
              
        • Strategic timing patterns: burstiness (clustering of responses), within‑task productivity
                  slopes (warm‑up vs fatigue), and "insight"‑like patterns such as long pauses followed by
                  rapid output.
          • 
              
        • Semantic trajectory features: distances in BERT embedding space, trajectory length and
                  efficiency, clustering of responses in semantic space, movement toward/away from the prompt,
                  and proximity to predefined "cognitive anchor" word sets (e.g., spatial or reasoning terms).
          • 
              
        • Phonetic & phonological features: phonetic embeddings from PWESuite, trajectory length and
                  clustering in phonological space, phoneme and syllable counts, and basic phonological
                  complexity indices.
          • 
              
        • Archetypal patterns: coverage and switching among learned "archetype" regions in semantic
                  and phonetic space (archetype count, switches, span, and richness), summarizing the overall
                  breadth and organization of search.
          • 
              
        • Task adherence & rule violations: counts of repetitions, prompt repeats, non‑responses,
                  or off‑task comments; these are excluded from most feature computations but are retained as
                  quality indicators.
          • 
              
        • Extended‑task features: sentence‑repetition bigram accuracy, visual and auditory reaction
                  times, and reading speed features when those tasks are present.
          • 
            
        

            
        Interpreting values
        
            
        
              ISS features are intended as research measures rather than clinical scores. They can capture
              small, domain‑specific associations with standardized tests (e.g., working memory, processing
              speed, or visuospatial reasoning), but they have not been validated as standalone diagnostic
              tools.
            
        
            
          
              
        • Higher word counts and faster timing often track general efficiency and processing speed, but
                  extreme values may also reflect strategy or task engagement.
          • 
              
        • Lexical maturity features (e.g., later age of acquisition, higher imageability) can indicate more
                  elaborated or abstract responses, especially in semantic fluency.
          • 
              
        • Semantic trajectory and archetype metrics describe how broadly and systematically participants
                  explore the space of possible responses, rather than "good" versus "bad" performance in a
                  clinical sense.
          • 
              
        • Reaction‑time and sentence‑repetition features have shown associations with processing speed and
                  working‑memory indices in research samples, but effect sizes are modest and sample‑dependent.
          • 
            
        
            
        
              When interpreting features, focus on patterns across related measures (e.g., a cluster of timing
              features or several semantic‑trajectory metrics) rather than any single value in isolation.
            
        

            
        Output files
        
            
        
              Pipeline outputs are written under each participant’s output folder. Key files include:
            
        
            
          
              
        • review_files/<ID>_cleaned_transcription.tsv: cleaned, prompt‑aligned transcript used as
                  input to feature extraction.
          • 
              
        • features/<ID>_per_prompt.rds: per‑prompt feature table (one row per prompt).
          • 
              
        • features/<ID>_per_task.rds: features aggregated within each task type.
          • 
              
        • features/<ID>_per_participant.rds: single‑row summary of all tasks for that participant.
          • 
              
        • features/<ID>_all_features.rds: a wide table containing the full feature set.
          • 
            
        
            
        
              You can preview tabular outputs in the Results tab or open the folder to analyze them in R or
              Python using the code from the ISS analysis repository.
